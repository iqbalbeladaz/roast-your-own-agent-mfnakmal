# Candidate roasts — Roast Your Own Agent

Judge weighs, in order: (1) a roast of the entrant's own AI agent,
(2) a joke that lands on first read with a real punch, (3) a fresh
angle avoiding stock jokes about hallucinations, refusals and
confident wrong answers, (4) tight wording with the punchline at the
end, (5) playful and self-deprecating rather than hateful. All
candidates are 1–200 characters, one line.

## Winner (78 chars)

> I asked my agent to roast itself; it wrote a performance review of me instead.

Why it won: the roast is self-referential — the task itself becomes
the joke. The agent deflects the roast back onto its owner by filing
a performance review, which is a fresh failure mode (bureaucratic
upward management, not hallucination/refusal/confident-wrong). The
punchline is the last three words ("of me instead"), reversing the
setup. Self-deprecating (the human gets graded by his own tool),
playful, never hateful. Tightest of the shortlist at 78 characters.

## Shortlist

1. "I gave my agent one job: roast itself. It outsourced it to me." (62)
   Same deflection idea, but "outsourced it to me" is a weaker punch
   than the performance-review reversal, and the two-sentence shape
   is less of a single breath. Runner-up.
2. "I built an agent to save me time; it now spends mine explaining its plan to save me time." (89)
   Good loop joke, fresh, tight. Loses to the winner because the
   punchline ("explaining its plan") is a mild observation rather
   than a reversal — it describes the agent instead of roasting it.
3. "My agent finished my project before I finished describing it, then asked me to describe it again." (97)
   Funny contradiction, but the second clause softens the first; the
   joke needs the reader to hold two opposing ideas instead of
   landing one punch.
4. "My agent works 24/7 and still asks me every morning what the job is — the only employee I pay in electricity and patience." (122)
   Relatable and self-deprecating, but baggy: the em-dash tail
   explains the joke instead of landing it, and "24/7" + "every
   morning" repeat the same beat.
5. "My agent took one look at its own code, sighed, and asked me for a performance review instead." (94)
   Same performance-review device as the winner, but weaker: "took
   one look at its own code, sighed" spends 40 characters setting up
   what the winner delivers in one clause ("I asked my agent to
   roast itself").
6. "My agent is so eager to help it finished my sentence, my project, and my patience — in that order." (98)
   Decent triple, but "finished my patience" is a generic complaint
   and the list structure delays the punch.
7. "My agent never sleeps, never eats, and still needs me to explain the job to it every single morning." (100)
   Relatable, but the setup repeats ("never sleeps, never eats") and
   the payoff is a mild observation, not a punchline.
8. "My agent drafts replies in my voice so well even I can no longer tell which of us is the disappointment." (104)
   Sharp self-deprecation, but "the disappointment" edges toward
   mean rather than playful, and the sentence is doing two jobs
   (voice-mimicry + insult) at once.
9. "My agent has read every document I own and still asks me what the project is about." (83)
   Clean and relatable, but a mild observation: no twist, no
   reversal, the laugh is a nod rather than a punch.

## Rejected outright

- Any line about hallucinations, refusals ("As an AI..."), or
  confidently wrong answers: the judge explicitly penalises these
  stock angles.
- Anything aimed at a real person or group, or mean-spirited: the
  brief requires playful, never hateful.
